tunravel rule

prove you own an address

[info]

Exchanges now ask for cryptographic proof that you own your withdrawal address. That proof goes into a database next to your passport and home address, and on to their compliance and chain-analytics vendors. These databases leak, and a leak is a shopping list for wrench attackers: who holds bitcoin, how much, and where they sleep.

The usual ways to comply give away more. An xpub reveals your whole wallet and its history. A test payment links the address to your other coins on-chain. Signing with your cold wallet brings out the keys that guard everything, and many hardware wallets can't sign it.

The rule only asks you to prove you control the address you withdraw to, and any address you control will do.

This tool provides a hop address that does the signing instead. You withdraw to it, and it forwards everything to the destination. Cold keys stay cold: the destination signs nothing.

  • The exchange gets the bare minimum: one signature for one address, and one later payment from it to the destination, which it would have seen anyway. No xpub, balance or history ever enters its system. It still knows who you are; this only stops it prying into the rest of your wallet and your entire transaction history.
  • This system is designed so it can't take your coins, but that depends on the code this site serves being honest. So the safest way to use this tool is to download the browser client and run it on your own machine.
  • If you lose this browser, or the server vanishes, the key of the bc1p address you gave can still claim the funds with the emergency kit you saved. The source code's just rescue command builds the spend for Bitcoin Core from that kit and the wallet's seed words, offline.
  • There's no account or sign-up. The server sees which hop address goes to which bc1p address and, when depositing, to which exchange deposit address. It also sees the exchange's message you sign, your deposits and, like any website, your IP address.
  • No warranty. This is experimental, unaudited software, provided as is. The server can't move your coins on its own, and nobody will make good a loss: you use it at your own risk with small amounts.
  • There's no service fee. The destination receives the deposit minus the forward's network fee, which the page estimates before you withdraw.
